What other names does Wau Kikik have?

supported · names

The book also calls Wau Kikik “Wau Kaca” and uses “Laga Kite” in its heading. These names are not established as interchangeable in every context.
More detail

The English heading is “Wau Kikik or the Laga Kite”; the Malay heading is “Wau Kikik Atau Layang-layang Laga”. A heading links names within that passage, but does not prove that every use of Kikik and Laga refers to the same kite.

Why is it called Wau Kaca?

supported · name_reason

The book links the name “Wau Kaca” to powdered glass on the kite string, intended to defeat an opponent’s wau in competition. The Malay text explicitly describes cutting the opponent’s string. The glass concerns the string, not the kite’s body.
More detail

The English account describes defeating an opponent’s wau on contact; the Malay account names the intended cutting action. This historical explanation does not establish that every Wau Kikik uses glass-coated string or that the practice continues today.

How many people launch Wau Laga?

supported · launch

Two players launch Wau Laga: a launcher (juru lancar) and a puller (juru tarik). The book also names “juru anjong” and “juru main tali”, but does not explicitly map these labels to the later pair; they do not mean four players.
More detail

Page 144 first names juru anjong and juru main tali, then discusses the distance between juru lancar and juru tarik. The quoted passage requires two players; it does not provide an explicit one-to-one equivalence between the two pairs of labels.
